# Onboarding, delivery, retention, and expansion

## Sales-to-delivery handoff

Before customer kickoff, capture:

- Stakeholders and roles
- Purchase trigger and desired outcome
- Promised scope, exclusions, price/payment status, and special commitments
- Success criteria and acceptance method
- Timeline, customer dependencies, risks, and communication cadence
- The first measurable value event

Do not make the customer repeat information already learned in sales.

## Activation and onboarding

Define one verifiable first-value event tied to why the customer bought. Build the shortest path to it. The onboarding plan needs owners, dates, prerequisites, communication channel, escalation path, and an explicit exit gate.

Onboarding is complete when first value is evidenced, the success plan is shared, the steady-state owner is known, and open risks are recorded.

## Delivery control

- Convert scope into deliverables with third-party-testable acceptance criteria.
- Record customer inputs and dependencies separately from provider tasks.
- Any scope, price, or timeline change needs an explicit change record and approval.
- Track decisions, risks, blocked items, deliverable status, acceptance, invoice/payment status, and lessons learned.
- Do not mark delivery complete because work was sent; completion requires the agreed acceptance state or a documented exception.

## Value proof

At agreed milestones, compare the starting state, promised outcome, actual evidence, unresolved gap, and next success target. Use customer-confirmed evidence wherever possible. This review supplies renewal, case study, referral, and expansion material.

## Health and retention

Use a small set of leading signals suited to the business:

- Outcome/milestone progress
- Usage or adoption where relevant
- Engagement and responsiveness
- Support friction and unresolved risk
- Payment, commercial, or stakeholder changes
- Renewal confidence and next-value opportunity

Do not pretend a weighted health score is predictive until checked against real outcomes. Initially, show component signals and confidence.

## Save, renew, expand, refer

Diagnose the cause before choosing a response. Price, temporary timing, missing capability, poor fit, delivery failure, and stakeholder change require different actions. Do not default to discounting.

For renewal or expansion, prepare a value review, changed needs, remaining risks, recommended next outcome, commercial option, and mutual decision plan. Ask for a referral or case study only after specific value has been acknowledged and permission is explicit.
