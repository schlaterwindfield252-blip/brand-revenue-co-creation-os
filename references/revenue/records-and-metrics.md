# Records and metrics

## Canonical objects

Keep these objects linked by stable IDs:

1. **Account/contact** — identity, role, consent/contact preferences, history.
2. **Evidence item** — source, date, raw language, context, interpretation, confidence.
3. **Problem/opportunity hypothesis** — segment, situation, evidence, contradiction, next test.
4. **Offer/price version** — promise, scope, exclusions, value metric, price, cost, test dates.
5. **Sales opportunity** — stage, evidence, value, decision date, next step, result.
6. **Engagement/delivery** — scope, milestones, dependencies, acceptance, changes, payment state.
7. **Customer success record** — first value, outcome evidence, risks, renewal, expansion, referral.

Do not place sensitive personal data in AI prompts unless the user has an approved handling method. Minimize data and anonymize research material when possible.

## Minimal KPI tree

Use counts and rates only when the denominator and period are explicit.

- Demand: qualified problem signals, validation experiments, evidence strength.
- Sales: qualified opportunities, stage conversion, cycle time, win/loss/no-decision reasons, next-step hygiene.
- Economics: realized price, contribution margin, acquisition effort/cost, payback, capacity consumed.
- Delivery: time to first value, milestone/acceptance status, scope changes, on-time payment.
- Success: outcome achievement, retention/repeat purchase, expansion, referrals, risk reasons.

Start with a small scorecard that drives a decision. Add a metric only when someone will take a defined action from it.

## Review cadence

- After every customer interaction: update evidence, stage, next action, and risk.
- Weekly: pipeline, delivery exceptions, customer risk, cash/payment, and three priorities.
- Monthly: win/loss, offer/pricing learning, unit economics, capacity, retention, and automation backlog.
- Quarterly or after a meaningful sample: recalibrate stages, conversion assumptions, health signals, and pricing.

## CRM choice

A spreadsheet is sufficient while it remains a reliable source of truth. Move to a CRM when history is fragmented, follow-ups are missed, multiple channels must synchronize, permissions matter, or automation value exceeds operating cost. Preserve IDs, definitions, stage evidence, and change history during migration.
