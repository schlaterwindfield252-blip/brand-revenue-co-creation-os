# Sales and CRM

## Canonical opportunity record

One opportunity per buying decision. Required fields:

- Stable ID, account, primary contact, source, owner
- Problem, consequence, desired outcome, current alternative
- Stakeholders and decision process
- Stage and stage evidence
- Estimated value and confidence basis
- Target decision date
- Last meaningful interaction
- One dated next action, including who must act
- Risks, objections, loss reason, and linked evidence/documents

## Default stages

| Stage | Exit evidence |
|---|---|
| New signal | A named account/contact and a plausible reason to engage |
| Discovery scheduled | Buyer accepted a substantive conversation |
| Qualified | Problem, consequence, authority/participants, process, timing, and next step documented |
| Solution aligned | Buyer confirmed success criteria and fit; material risks are known |
| Proposal active | Buyer acknowledged scope/price and a decision plan exists |
| Verbal/approval | Explicit intent exists; remaining approval, legal, procurement, or payment steps are named |
| Closed won/lost | Signed/paid/explicitly accepted, or a recorded loss/no-decision reason |

Customize stages only when the actual buying process differs. Probabilities should come from observed conversion by stage when enough history exists; before then, use qualitative confidence instead of fake precision.

## Hygiene rules

- No open opportunity without a dated next action and target decision date.
- A next step must name a concrete action and responsible party; “follow up” is insufficient.
- Flag inactivity and stage age relative to the company's observed cycle, not universal day thresholds.
- Record no-decision and loss reasons; do not silently delete failed opportunities.
- Keep forecast category separate from stage.

## Proposal gate

Do not produce a final proposal without buyer-specific problem language, success criteria, scope, exclusions, price basis, decision process, and urgency/trigger. A proposal should contain:

- Buyer-specific executive summary
- Problem and desired outcome
- Approach and scope
- Explicit exclusions and customer responsibilities
- Deliverables with testable acceptance criteria
- Timeline, dependencies, and milestones
- Price and payment structure approved by the owner
- Change-control process
- Evidence/proof appropriate to the claim
- One decision path with dated mutual next steps

Do not invent binding legal clauses. Separate the persuasive proposal/SOW from legal review and signature documents.

## Weekly pipeline review

Return: data defects, stalled opportunities, decisions blocked, prioritized next actions, forecast assumptions, and lessons from wins/losses. Do not inflate the pipeline with unqualified conversations.
