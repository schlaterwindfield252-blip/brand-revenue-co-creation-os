# Customer validation

Use for interviews, surveys, reviews, communities, support logs, win/loss notes, and sales calls.

## Evidence ledger

Capture before synthesizing:

- Source and date
- Segment and buying situation
- Raw phrase or exact quote
- Signal type: trigger, struggle, desired progress, objection, alternative, outcome, risk
- Person's role: buyer, user, influencer, blocker
- Intensity: casual, active workaround, active search, budgeted, urgent
- Evidence strength: isolated, repeated, quantified, paid behavior, high-fit account
- Interpretation and confidence

Keep observations distinct from interpretations. Preserve contradictions.

## Interview discipline

Prefer past behavior and concrete events over opinions about hypothetical features. Ask about:

- What happened immediately before the search began?
- How was the problem handled last time?
- What did the workaround cost in time, money, risk, or attention?
- What alternatives were considered or purchased?
- Who cared, who decided, and what almost stopped the decision?
- What would count as a meaningful successful outcome?

Do not pitch during discovery. Do not treat politeness, feature enthusiasm, or “I would pay” as purchase evidence.

## Validation decision

For each problem hypothesis, report:

| Hypothesis | Supporting evidence | Contradicting evidence | Confidence | Cheapest next test |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Valid next tests may include another targeted interview, a paid diagnostic, a manual concierge service, a deposit/preorder, a letter of intent, or a narrowly scoped pilot. Choose the least expensive test that could realistically disprove the hypothesis.

## Output

Produce an evidence ledger, meaningful behavior-based segments, JTBD statements linked to evidence, opportunity areas, open questions, and the next validation experiment. Sparse evidence must lead to lower confidence, not stronger storytelling.
